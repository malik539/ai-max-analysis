What AI Max actually bought

82.2% of AI Max spend went to queries naming a specific practice, provider, or street address — against 28.5% of keyword-matched spend. 249 terms were bare street addresses with no service word at all, costing $338. Its vocabulary skews to “dental” (957 uses) and “dentist” (388) over all orthodontic terms combined (574): on-service for one general dental practice, off-service for four orthodontists.

The conversions AI Max did produce are not all real leads

Of 17.64 AI Max conversions, only 4.64 came from highly relevant searches. The rest include a hospital lookup (sutter health hospital sacramento, $23.61), a bare address (140 roseville pkwy suite 150…, $28.20) and, at Ortho Excellence, six conversions from general-dental searches (dentist tacoma, dental clinic near me) on an account that sells orthodontics. Incrementality: six of the 17 converting terms contained no word the account already reached through keywords — the rest is duplicated intent, and the “new” vocabulary is almost entirely competitors’ proper nouns. AI Max found new names, not new demand.

Where the AI Max money went

Do this next

  • Ortho Excellence — restrict hard, this week. 73.5% of search spend at 8.3% relevance. Turn AI Max off in the Puyallup/Bonney Lake campaign, or add brand exclusions plus a general-dentistry negative list before the next billing cycle.
  • Holt — add an address/numeric negative pattern now. 205 address terms cost $297 for one questionable conversion. Then negate non-dental specialties: veneers, endodontics, wisdom teeth, optometry.
  • Deploy one shared negative list across all four ortho accounts — general dentistry, other dental specialties, non-dental medical, DTC aligner brands, address patterns. It addresses most of the $920 waste.
  • Turn on brand exclusions — 82.2% of AI Max spend is competitor and address traffic.
  • Fix conversion tracking before the next review: no values or action names means nobody can tell a booked consult from a form start.
  • Re-measure in 60–90 days. Do not disable Goodman or Sabinsky on 10–13 clicks.

Three limits on this data

1 · Google withholds 29–45% of the spend

Named terms cover only 55–71% of each account’s search spend; the rest sits in the privacy-thresholded “Other search terms” bucket, attributable to neither AI Max nor keywords. Holt is weakest — 45% of its conversions are invisible.

2 · No conversion values or action names

One blended Conversions column, so booked consults cannot be separated from form starts. Treat every conversion here as a lead signal of unknown quality.

3 · One 30-day window, 267 AI Max clicks

Ample for relevance conclusions, thin for efficiency ones. Goodman ($112) and Sabinsky ($96) are too small to judge on performance at all.
